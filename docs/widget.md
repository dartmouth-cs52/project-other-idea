# How the widget follows a page

The widget runs on pages it doesn't control. This covers its chat, and how it keeps up with those pages: dialogs, controls something else covers, single-page apps that change the address, what it learns about a site from visits, and how to test it in real browsers. For the project overview, see the [README](../README.md).

## The chat

The chat is the one in the home page's demo (`web/components/demo/kit.tsx`), drawn by `src/widget/ui.ts`. It stays open from the question to the last step. The question is a bubble at the top. One line under it says what Haptic is doing: "Thinking", "Showing you how" with the pointer mark, or the green tick and "Done". The ask box's button sends the question, and while Haptic is thinking or pointing it is the stop button. Under the ask box, always, is the line that says Haptic is an AI and can be wrong.

**The pointer.** It glides to the control from where it was, or from the chat if it was out of sight, in 400 to 700ms depending on how far it goes. The mark and the caption show when it lands. A click on the control is answered with a press: the mark and the pointer dip and come back in a quarter of a second, where they are, even if the click took the control off the page. Then the mark and the caption go, and the pointer waits there for the next step. Filling in a field, or clicking something else, gets no press.

**Never on top of the control.** When the chat would cover the control being pointed at, or its caption, it closes for that step and comes back when the pointer leaves. For that step the caption carries a small × that stops the guide. It steps aside once for a control, so a visitor who opens the chat again keeps it open.

**How it ends.** A sentence that ends a guide is a reply in the chat. The green tick and "Done" are for a guide the visitor followed to the end, which is the same ending that is billed (`finished`). An answer in words, a control that was shown and not used, and a sentence that says Haptic can't help end without it. "Did this help?" follows, and the answer is posted to `/v1/guides/<id>/report` and kept on the guide (`guides.helped`), where the dashboard counts it.

**The thinking glow.** Thinking shows on the page, not in the chat. While the widget waits for the model, the accent colour gathers along the top edge of the page over 0.6 seconds and laps there. When the reply arrives, which is when the pointer sets off or the answer shows, it breaks away as a soft band the width of the page and falls to the bottom in 2.45 seconds, turning violet and then pink, and is gone. A reply that says something went wrong, and a guide that was stopped, fade it where it is. The glow always finishes gathering first, and nothing waits for that. Its path and timings are measured from the animation it is modelled on.

It is drawn by an element of its own, `<haptic-veil>`, which sits in the page just before `<haptic-root>`, so the chat, the pointer, the mark and the caption are over it and are never tinted. Being a separate element is what lets it be blended into the page: multiplied into a light page, so the page's words stay dark and sharp through it, and screened into a dark one, going by the backgrounds behind three spots on the page. It takes no clicks, the snapshot and the learning skip it (`data-haptic-ignore`), and it follows `<haptic-root>` into a page's modal dialog. It is not displayed at all unless a glow is on, and that is switched in its own stylesheet, so the page's scripts see nothing change. Inside it are three layers of radial gradients with only their opacity and transform animated. The colours are worked out from the site's accent, so a site with its own accent gets a glow to match. With reduced motion on there is no glow, no shimmer and no glide. `test/browser/chat.test.ts` covers all of this.

## Modal dialogs

A page's modal `<dialog>` makes everything outside it inert, Haptic's own element included. The widget moves its element into the open dialog the moment it opens, shows it again on top, and moves it back out when the dialog closes, so the pointer and the chat's stop button keep working through nested dialogs too. Escape in Haptic's ask box closes Haptic's panel and not the page's dialog.

## Covered controls

A control on screen that something else covers, like a button under a stack of cards or the page behind a full-screen overlay, is left out of the snapshot, because a visitor can't click it. The browser's own hit test decides (`src/widget/occlusion.ts`). A control only scrolled out of its scroll box stays in, and so does one under a sticky header, since pointing scrolls it to the middle of the screen.

## Route changes

Single-page apps change the address without loading a page, so the widget watches for it itself: through the browser's Navigation API, or by looking at the address every tenth of a second in a browser without it, and never by changing the page's own `history` (`src/widget/navigation.ts`). If the address changes while the model is answering, the answer is about a page the visitor has left, like the page they were on when they clicked something that took a moment to load. The widget hangs up at once and asks again from the new page, and the server stops the model call and counts the dropped step on the guide (`cancelled_steps`). An app that changes the address first and draws the page a moment later is waited for: a new address over the same controls as before has not been drawn yet.

## Learning the site from visits

The crawl builds the first map of a site, but it can't sign in, and it can't open what only a click reveals. So the widget also sends an outline of each page it loads on, once per page per tab, when the page has settled and the browser is idle (`src/widget/learn.ts`): the title, headings, and the labels of its links and buttons, with an icon-only button named by its icon, like `icon:share-2`. When a click is followed by a new page within eight seconds, it sends the clicked label as a link on the page the click came from, like "View, in a dialog → /set/:id", which is how Haptic learns a page that only a dialog leads to. Labels only: never what is typed into a field, never what is in a table's cells, and a label holding an email address or a long number is dropped whole.

Each label is counted by distinct visitor (`src/server/learned.ts`). The widget keeps a random id in the site's `localStorage`, and the server keeps only a hash of it made with the site's id, up to ten per label, in `outline_labels`. A browser that keeps no storage has no id, and what it sends is not used: a hash of a network address could be turned back into the address. A label reaches the prompt only once enough different visitors have seen it: 3 online, 1 on your own machine, or `HAPTIC_LABEL_THRESHOLD`. That keeps one person's own data, like the name of their project, out of everyone's prompt. What is learned is merged with the crawl's pages when a site is read from the database, so a step never waits on it. The crawl's pages come first, pages only traffic knows follow, navigation that is on most pages is listed once, and each page lists at most 24 buttons. Learned labels are listed in a fixed order, so a count going up does not change the prompt the model provider keeps cached. A site whose context has `"learn": false` sends no outlines.

**What menus show.** When a visitor opens a menu, a tab or an accordion, the widget notes the links and buttons on screen as the control is pressed, looks again several times over about a second and a half until the next press, and sends whatever appeared in any look with the control's label, like `Help` opening `Help center → /help; Keyboard shortcuts; What's new; Contact support`. It is watched from the press and not the click, because menus like Radix's open on the press, and it looks more than once because some people pick from a menu within a few hundred milliseconds. Nothing a field suggests is sent, since suggestions follow what was typed, and nothing that opens from a table's cells. Each control is sent once per page per tab. The server counts each item by distinct visitor in `outline_openings` (`db/migrations/0012`), the same way as page labels, and adds the items enough visitors saw to the page's `opens`, the field the dashboard's reader and the mapper fill in too. Then two things use it: the site map lists `behind "Help": …` for each page, and on the page the visitor is on, a closed control is listed with what it opens, like `e7 button "Help" (collapsed) [header] opens: Help center → /help; Contact support`. A control in the header, navigation or a sidebar gets what it showed on other pages, as long as that was the same everywhere it was seen. One in the main part of the page gets only its own page's, since "More actions" on two pages can open different things.

**On your own machine.** Without a database, what is learned is kept in `data/learned.json`, which `.gitignore` leaves out, so restarting the server doesn't forget the site. Delete the file to start again.

## Putting the launcher away

A visitor who doesn't want the launcher in the corner can minimize it. A small button with an × shows on the launcher's outer bottom corner while the mouse is over it or it has focus, and turns it into a dot. The dot opens up a little to show the pointer when the mouse is over it, and a click brings the launcher back. The choice is kept in the site's `localStorage` as `haptic.minimized`, so it holds on the next page. Where storage is blocked, the launcher is minimized on that page only. `test/browser/minimize.test.ts` covers it in all three browsers.

## Testing in browsers

`npm run test:browser` runs the widget in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, on small pages made for each test, against a stand-in server that answers each step from a script (`test/browser/harness.ts`). It needs Playwright's browsers once: `npx playwright install chromium firefox webkit`. `BROWSERS=chromium npm run test:browser` runs one. On a machine that has Chromium but not Playwright's own download of it, `CHROMIUM_PATH=/path/to/chrome` runs the Chromium tests in that browser.
