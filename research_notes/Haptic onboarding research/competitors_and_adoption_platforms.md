## 1. What is the real competitive set for "AI that guides a user through a live web app UI by pointing or highlighting", and what is each company's status in November 2026?

### Takeaway
The closest live competitors to a "user asks, a pointer shows the next element, the user clicks" guide are **Jimo's Adoption Agent** (documented in detail: reads the visible page's text and interactive elements, shows "a cursor and a tooltip on one control at a time; the user clicks") and **Frigade Assistant / Assist API** ("highlights the next click and walks the workflow, across pages and modals"); **Product Fruits' Elvin** and a tail of small entrants (Skippr, Invocursor, Fluidworks, Orango) are adjacent. Several earlier entrants are gone or have moved away: **Layup** is marked Inactive by YC, **Tandem**'s site now sells an implementation-team workspace instead of an in-app copilot, **Sammy Labs** has repositioned, **Command AI** was absorbed into Amplitude Guides and Surveys, and **Usetiful** was bought by Fullstory.

### Inferences
- The live, documented, self-serve competitor closest to a "type a question, a pointer shows the next control, the user clicks" product is Jimo's Adoption Agent. Frigade is the closest in ambition (it also re-learns the app on its own) but is priced and sold at a higher tier.
- The churn among the pure "AI cursor" startups (Layup inactive, Tandem and Sammy repositioned within about 18 months of launch) suggests the standalone category has been hard to sustain; the survivors bundled the pointer into a broader suite (Jimo, Product Fruits) or sell an API to teams that already have an agent (Frigade Assist API). This is my reading of the pattern, not something any source states.
- Incumbent DAPs (Pendo, Appcues, Chameleon, Userpilot, Whatfix) have mostly spent their AI effort on the **authoring side** (draft a guide from a prompt, tag features, repair selectors), not on runtime pointing in response to an end user's question.
- Most new entrants act for the user by default (Tandem, Invocursor, Skippr, page-agent). "Never clicks for the user" is a minority position; Jimo's Guide Skills and Skippr's "guide-only mode" are the nearest equivalents.

## 2. Is each product self-serve or sales-led, what are the signup steps, and what does it cost?

## 3. What does the customer have to install, and when in the flow is it asked for?

### Takeaway
Every embedded product needs a JS snippet (or npm package) on every page plus a user-identify call, and almost all DAPs add a Chrome extension for the builder. The meaningful difference is ordering: Jimo, Chameleon, Product Fruits, UserGuiding and Userflow let the non-developer start with the extension and defer the snippet, while Userpilot, Appcues, Pendo and Amplitude put the developer install first.

## 5. How does each product know the app's UI, what breaks when the UI changes, and what self-healing or AI claims are made?

### Takeaway
The established platforms store a CSS selector per step, captured by a human in a browser-extension builder, and their own documentation admits this breaks on dynamic class names and UI changes; their AI work is aimed at generating and repairing those selectors. The AI-native products split into four different learning strategies: a human-supervised scan of logged-in pages plus live page reading (Jimo), an autonomous browser agent with a test login (Frigade), reading the source repository (Candu, Pendo Novus), and reading the live page at question time with no prior learning (Tandem as launched, page-agent).

### Inferences
- Our runtime design (read role, label and state of interactive elements live; one LLM call per step; user clicks) is the same family as Jimo's "Read all" mode. - Our weak point relative to the field is prior knowledge of the logged-in app. A plain-HTTP, no-login crawl sees none of the screens behind authentication. Competitors close that gap in three ways, each implying a different onboarding step: (a) the admin walks the app once in their own browser with an extension or overlay (Jimo scan, Userflow Smartflow, Candu Capture Run); (b) the customer provides a test login and an agent explores (Frigade); (c) the customer connects a GitHub repo (Candu, Pendo Novus).

### Inferences
- There are three onboarding shapes in the market, set by how the product learns:
  1. *Nothing but the tag* (Tandem as launched, Invocursor, Skippr "out of the box"): shortest flow, weakest prior knowledge.
  2. *Admin demonstrates* (Jimo, Layup, Userflow, Candu Capture Run): needs an extension or overlay and 3 to 30 minutes of an admin's time, yields human-vetted knowledge of logged-in screens, and works before any code is installed.
  3. *Hand over access* (Frigade test login; Candu and Pendo Novus GitHub): longest security conversation, least customer effort afterwards, automatic re-learning.
- Our current inputs (public crawl plus supplied notes and docs) put us in shape 1 with a little of Jimo's public-site read. Moving to shape 2 costs an extension; moving to shape 3 costs a credentials vault and a headless-browser crawler.
- Frigade's recommendation to train on staging is a practical answer to the "do not let a bot click around production" objection, and a test login is something an early-stage customer can create in a minute. It is also the only approach that keeps knowledge fresh without customer effort.

## 7. How do vendors verify that the install succeeded?

### Takeaway
The best pattern found is a three-part status readout (script installed, initialised, user identified) surfaced both in the dashboard and in a browser-extension debugger, backed by a checklist step that ticks itself. Userflow, Appcues and Jimo do this well; Pendo warns data "can take up to two hours".

### Cited Findings
- **Userflow** debugger (opened from the Chrome extension) shows three checks: **Installation** ("Installed", or an error naming the missing snippet and current URL), **Initialization** ("Initialized") and **User identification** (User ID, Company Name, Environment, Token, or a warning with links). A quick check is to sort the Users tab by Last Seen. If `userflow.js` loads but no WebSocket opens, either `identify()` isn't called on all pages or a firewall or CSP is blocking. **[fetched]** — [Userflow docs](https://help.userflow.com/docs/userflowjs-installation-check.md)
- **Appcues**: in **Settings > Web** your domains appear under "Connected domains" with the status "Installed"; in the debugger "SDK Loaded" shows a green checkmark, with further rows for "User Identified", events, pages, Key Moments and elements. **[fetched]** — [Appcues quick start](https://docs.appcues.com/getting-started/quick-start-guide-for-appcues)

- **Userflow's AI install skill** ends with its own verification: deploy, sign in as a test user, confirm the user appears in Userflow, and run `userflow.isIdentified()` on a signed-out page expecting `false`. **[fetched]** — [Userflow docs](https://help.userflow.com/docs/install-with-ai)

### Inferences
- Our product can verify more quickly than analytics-first tools because the widget's first server call is itself proof of install; a "detected on yourdomain.com" state can flip within seconds, where Pendo warns of up to two hours.

### Inferences

**Ranked walkthrough list and what to look for**

1. **Jimo** (highest value; closest to our product and the most complete self-serve flow).
   - *Signup:* which fields are asked beyond product URL and goal; how long the "reading your product" step takes; what the failure state looks like when it "could not read your product".

   - *Developer step:* the exact text behind "Share Instructions to Engineer" and the coding-agent prompt; how fast "detected on your domain" flips; whether the trial visibly extends by two weeks.
2. **Frigade** (the alternative learning model).
   - Start with the public demo to see "highlights the next click" across pages and modals, and note whether the user or the assistant clicks.
   - In the signup, look for how the URL and test login are requested, what assurances are given about credentials, whether staging is suggested, how training progress and the learned map are shown, how Skills are reviewed and enabled, and where "manual retrain" lives.
   - Note the point at which a human from Frigade gets involved; the flow may not be fully self-serve at $1,000/month.
3. **Userflow** (install verification and handoff craft).
   - Preview a flow on your own app with only the extension; open the debugger on a page with no snippet and read the error copy; copy the developer instructions and the AI-install prompt; record a Smartflow to see how a single demonstration becomes steps and where element selection fails.
4. **Product Fruits** (explicit fork between extension and snippet, low price).
   - Watch how the setup wizard words the choice, what "Full preview" shows, how the limitations of extension mode are disclosed, and how Elvin is configured (knowledge sources, "annotate key product areas") and whether it launches a tour or draws one live.
5. **Pendo, including Novus** (incumbent baseline and the repo-access path).
   - See whether a new account can still start on Free; how visitor and account IDs are explained to a non-developer; how the two-hour data delay is communicated; and, in Novus, what permissions the GitHub connection requests and what it produces in the first 30 minutes.

**Worth a shorter look if time allows**
- **Chameleon**: build with the extension before install, then look at Ranger's "detached element" repair flow.
- **UserGuiding**: the "UG Now" extension as a way to put content live on a page without touching code, and its CSP limitation.
- **Skippr AI**: an AI-native flow with free credits and a meeting-link mode that needs no install.
- **Amplitude Guides and Surveys**: a counter-example where everything depends on the SDK being installed first.

**Design implications to carry into the onboarding work (my synthesis)**
- Put an extension-based "try it on your own logged-in app" step before the script tag, as Jimo, Product Fruits, Chameleon, Userflow and UserGuiding do.
- Use the same extension session to let the admin walk a few logged-in screens so the product learns them, which addresses the gap left by a no-login crawl.
- Offer a test-login route (Frigade's model) as a later, optional upgrade for customers who want automatic re-learning.
- Make the developer step a shareable page with a manual snippet and a coding-agent prompt, show live detection status, and reward completion (trial extension).
- Keep the user-identify call optional at first; every incumbent makes it mandatory and it is the hardest part of their install.
